Calibration Checker

Say a weather app tells you there is a 70% chance of rain. If it is being honest, it should actually rain on about 70 out of every 100 days it says that. That property is called calibration: predicted probabilities matching what actually happens over time. It is a different thing from accuracy. A model can pick the right outcome most of the time and still hand out probabilities that are consistently too confident or too cautious.

This tool checks that. Upload a CSV with a column of predicted probabilities and a column of actual outcomes, and it draws a reliability diagram and computes a Brier score for you, right here in your browser. Nothing gets uploaded anywhere, everything runs in JavaScript on your machine.

How to use it

  1. Load the demo data below, or upload your own CSV.
  2. Check the column mapping the app guesses for you, and fix it if needed.
  3. Pick a bin count and hit Analyze.

What your CSV needs

ColumnRequiredWhat it should contain
predicted_prob yes A number between 0 and 1: the probability your model or forecaster assigned to the outcome.
outcome yes 0 or 1: whether that outcome actually happened.
segment optional A category label, if you want to compare calibration across subgroups (up to 6 groups).

Column names do not need to match exactly. You will get to remap them after loading the file. One row per prediction, please, not per underlying event.

What is the Brier score

The Brier score is the mean squared error between the predicted probability and the actual outcome (0 or 1). It is a single number that summarizes how good a set of probabilities is, the same way MSE summarizes a regression. 0 means perfect, and 0.25 is what a coin-flip guess of 50% gets you when the true rate is 50/50. Full definition on Wikipedia.

What we actually want to show is not the score alone, but where it breaks down. A model can have a decent overall Brier score and still be badly wrong for a chunk of its predictions, most commonly the ones it is most confident about. The reliability diagrams and the segment breakdown below are built to catch exactly that.

Load your data

Use the built-in demo, or upload your own file. The demo is 11,400 real observations: bookmaker implied probabilities from 10 seasons of Premier League matches (2015/16 to 2024/25), checked against whether each outcome (home win, draw, away win) actually happened. The segment column splits matches by the strength of the favorite (Strong, Mid, Weak), based on final league position that season. Full methodology in the notebook.
